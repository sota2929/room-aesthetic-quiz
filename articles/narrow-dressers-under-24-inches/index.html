Narrow Dressers Under 24 Inches Wide

The cabinet may fit the wall and still fail the room. Use outside width, depth, open-drawer space, and real storage needs to choose the right format.

The quick answer

For a wall opening under 26 inches, target a dresser no more than 24 inches wide so trim and installation do not erase your margin. A depth under 16 inches protects more walkway, but shallower drawers hold less and may be less forgiving for bulky clothes.

Best default: a five- or six-drawer vertical chest with published outside and drawer dimensions, an included anti-tip kit, and enough open floor to use every drawer.

  1. Measure at floor, middle, and top: old walls and baseboards can narrow the slot.
  2. Add the drawer extension: request or estimate it from the product diagram—cabinet depth alone is incomplete.
  3. Test the route: an open drawer should not trap the bedroom door or block required egress.
  4. Check delivery: confirm the assembled box can turn through your doorway, hall, and stairs.

Useful check: compare internal drawer dimensions when available. Two 22-inch-wide chests can have meaningfully different storage because of side thickness, runners, and frame structure.

Do not skip the last step

Tall and narrow makes anchoring more important

Follow the manufacturer’s anti-tip instructions and your lease rules. Keep heavier items low, open one drawer at a time, and never let children climb or hang on drawers. A wall attachment is a safety component—not optional styling hardware.

Wall material

Use hardware appropriate to the wall and follow the product instructions.

Baseboard gap

A thick baseboard can stop the chest from sitting as the anchor design expects.

Uneven floor

Level the unit according to instructions before loading it.

Rental restrictions

Ask the property owner about safe mounting; do not improvise a weaker substitute.
