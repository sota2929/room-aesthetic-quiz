Small Bedroom With a Desk and Dresser

You can keep a real work surface and real clothing storage—if both pieces stay shallow and the chair does not become permanent hallway furniture. Start with the plan that matches your room shape.

The quick answer

Put the desk and dresser on the same wall when the room is long; separate them into opposite corners when the room is square. Keep each piece around 18 inches deep. A desk chair needs to tuck completely under the top, while a dresser needs enough space for its deepest drawer plus your body.

The sizes that make this possible

Choose depth before width

Width decides how much storage or worktop you get. Depth decides whether you can still cross the room. Measure the actual bed frame—not only the mattress—and tape every furniture footprint on the floor.

Protect the room

Three mistakes that erase the floor space

  1. Buying by width alone. A deep desk plus a pulled-out chair can consume most of a small aisle.
  2. Ignoring open drawers. Tape the dresser body and its deepest drawer in the open position.
  3. Making both pieces heavy. Pair one closed storage piece with an open-leg desk to reduce visual weight.
